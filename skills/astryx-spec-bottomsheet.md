# BottomSheet component contract

## Intent

BottomSheet presents caller-provided content in a panel that rises from the
bottom edge. This record covers consumer anatomy, the native dialog every sheet
renders, keyboard-reachable scrolling, theming reachability, the Content area's
container padding, and how standalone sheets relate to one another: modal
sheets form one stack per document ordered by when they opened, a sheet that
closes in the same update another opens is replaced by it with one handoff
motion, and non-modal sheets are hosted in the browser's top layer (DEC-2).
Multi-step flows are written as one standalone sheet per step driven by one
state value; `BottomSheetSwitcher` is the deprecated predecessor of that
pattern (`component:BottomSheetSwitcher` DEP-0015). Where the shipped sheet
does not yet meet a requirement, the row carries a present-tense implementation
gap note.

## Compatibility and migration

- Released default preserved: `yes`, except the block-start inset; with no
  `padding` prop and no theme padding, the Content area insets by
  `--spacing-4` on the other logical edges, and `padding={0}` keeps them
  unpadded. The block-start inset is never less than the grab bar's height
  (`--spacing-6`), so a default sheet's content starts 8px lower, and a
  `padding={0}` sheet's content and a lone Section start below the bar
- Touch release (FR19): a touch release follows UIKit's sheet. A slow or
  medium touch throw travels less than it did, and a slow touch drag closes
  the sheet only past halfway from the lowest detent to closed, where it
  closed past 40 %. A mouse or pen drag is unchanged.
- Compatibility class: no public prop, type, default, or export changes.
  Stacking, the replace handoff, and non-modal top-layer hosting are behavior
  of the existing component, tagged `[feat]` (`spec:AST-017/FR7`: a compatible
  capability) and admissible in a patch under `spec:AST-017/FR25` as
  additive — no released contract described two presented sheets, so the stack
  gives behavior to a state no contract covered — and as
  implementation-equivalent for a sheet on its own: a modal sheet presented on
  its own behaves as released, except that it focuses an eligible
  `data-autofocus` descendant on open in every engine (AR4), a `[fix]` under
  `component:TextInput` FR2 and `component:Dialog` FR2; and a non-modal sheet
  on its own keeps its documented contract (no scrim, no scroll lock, page
  interactive, dismissal by `purpose`, `finalFocusRef` focused after every
  close, the element focused when it was shown focused again when it closes
  while focus is inside it, and focus on open on an eligible
  `data-autofocus` descendant, else where that engine's `show()` focusing
  steps put it — the panel in Chromium, the first control in Firefox)
  while its host changes from `show()` with a page-level `z-index` to the top
  layer with none (FR11). One exception, visual only: under
  `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` a lone sheet's entrance and exit follow
  FR15 (no transform travel; a cross-fade on a coarse pointer or after a
  touch drag's release, an immediate change otherwise) where the released
  motion ran its transforms shortened — a motion change under a preference,
  no change to any state, event, or focus outcome. Two modal sheets open at
  once stack (FR5) instead of presenting as unrelated dialogs that each dim
  the page
- Controlled/uncontrolled behavior: unchanged; `isOpen` stays fully controlled,
  and a sheet never writes another sheet's state (FR8)
- Migration decision: none for callers of BottomSheet. Flows written with
  `BottomSheetSwitcher` migrate to standalone steps under
  `component:BottomSheetSwitcher` DEP-0015

Consumer migration instructions belong in consumer docs and release notes.

## Ownership boundary

**Owns**

- The visual Sheet panel and its current `bottom-sheet` target.
- The scrolling Content area, its shared viewport/content-box integration, and
  automatic keyboard-path selection.
- The Content area's container padding, published per
  `architecture:container-padding`.
- Standalone sheet presentation, including its optional native-dialog Scrim.
- The native dialog every sheet renders, standalone or switcher-managed
  (`component:BottomSheetSwitcher` DEC-1).
- The stack of presented modal sheets in one document: their order, which one
  is on top, how far a covered sheet recedes, which backdrop paints the one
  scrim, and the native top-layer order that realizes it (FR5–FR7, DEC-2).
- The replace handoff between a sheet closing and a sheet opening in the same
  update (FR9–FR10).
- The top-layer hosting of a non-modal sheet (FR11).

**Does not own / non-goals**

- Caller-provided content rendered inside the Content area.
- A separate target for Content area, Handle, or Scrim; none exists on current
  `main`.
- The presentation lifecycle of its dialog and the flow's scrim ownership when
  the sheet participates in a BottomSheetSwitcher — owned by BottomSheetSwitcher
  (`component:BottomSheetSwitcher` DEC-1). The flow as a whole is one level of
  the stack (FR12).
- Shared layer hosting or dismissal policy. The Escape and platform-close order
  is `family:overlay-dismissal`'s; the stack only tells it which sheet can
  take a close request (FR7).
- Which `Dialog`, `Drawer`, `Popover`, or other layer paints above a sheet;
  those keep their own hosting (`architecture:layer-runtime`). A `Dialog`
  opened from a sheet is not a stack level.

## Public concepts

No new public concept is introduced. Stacking and the replace handoff are
behavior of the existing `isOpen` / `onOpenChange` contract: a sheet opened
while another modal sheet is open stacks on it; a sheet that closes in the same
update as one other sheet opens, from a control inside it, is replaced by it.
There is no stack component, context, or opt-in prop. Consumer props and usage
remain documented in `BottomSheet.doc.mjs`.

### Allowed variation

- Caller-provided children may vary without becoming BottomSheet-owned anatomy.
- Height, snap points, motion phase, and standalone versus switcher hosting may
  vary without changing the four-part anatomy recorded here.
- The recede geometry of a covered sheet (its scale and offset per depth), the
  handoff motion curve, and the order in which the entering and alignment
  motions of a replace complete may vary under theme motion tokens while FR6
  and FR10's ordering and inertness hold.

### Representative states

- A standalone modal sheet renders Sheet panel, Content area, Handle, and Scrim.
- A standalone non-modal sheet renders Sheet panel, Content area, and Handle
  without a Scrim.
- A switcher-managed sheet renders the same panel anatomy in its own dialog while
  BottomSheetSwitcher coordinates that dialog's lifecycle and the flow's one Scrim
  (`component:BottomSheetSwitcher` DEC-1).
- Stacked: two or more modal sheets presented in one document; the top sheet is
  interactive, each covered sheet is receded and blocked, one Scrim is visible.
- Replacing: a retained sheet and an entering sheet both presented through the
  handoff, the retained one inert — still painting the Scrim when it is the
  bottom open sheet (FR6), transparent when a covered sheet beneath owns it —
  then only the entering sheet.
- Non-modal: a sheet presented as a manual popover in the top layer with no
  Scrim; the page remains interactive.
- Reduced motion: the Stacked and Replacing states above with no transform
  motion — the covered sheet in its receded place at once, the entering and
  leaving sheets cross-fading on a coarse pointer and changing at once on any
  other, except a dismissal a touch drag's release started, which fades out in
  place on every pointer (FR15, `design:reduced-motion`).

### Transformation and precedence order

- Content area inset: the `padding` prop, then the theme's `padding` properties
  on `bottom-sheet`, then `--spacing-4` on every logical edge. At rest the
  block-start inset is never less than the grab bar's height (`--spacing-6`),
  so the first row starts below the bar; scrolled content still passes under it.
  That band under the bar is not container padding: the Content area publishes
  only the block-start padding past it (`max(padding - --spacing-6, 0)`) and
  applies the band on top, so a lone Section or a Layout that escapes its
  padding still starts below the bar. Every other edge applies and publishes
  the same inset.
- Stack order: open order, then tree order for sheets opening in one update
  (FR5). A sheet that closes and reopens takes a new place on top.
- Native top-layer order is derived from the stack order, not trusted: where
  the order the dialogs were shown in diverges from the stack order, the
  stack order holds in the first painted frame (FR5), and no dialog below the
  first divergence is presented again — a sheet whose place is already right
  fires no native `close`.
- A same-update close and open is tested against FR10 in that order of
  conditions; the first failing condition makes it a push and a pop.
- No new height rule is introduced. Its one gesture precedence rule is `spec:AST-094` FR14's named exception: at the tallest detent, a pull that carries the content past its end continues as the sheet's stretch (FR18).

### Performance and resources

- Element jobs (`architecture:component-style-authoring` INV7; the
  open-layer allowance of `spec:AST-017` FR60, DEC-29). An open sheet's frame
  has these elements, each with one job:
  - the dialog carries the sheet's dialog role, modality and the top layer
    when the sheet is modal, and hosts the panel at the bottom edge, so a
    percentage `height` is a share of the viewport; a covered sheet's recede
    (FR6) is this element's transform;
  - the panel paints the `bottom-sheet` target;
  - the grabber bar is the drag handle, and its `::before` draws the handle;
  - the body is the scroll area;
  - the content box carries the padding and is the observed content box (FR3);
  - the edge tint gives iOS Safari's toolbar the sheet's color: its own
    element outside the panel's transform, so it stays flush with the
    viewport's bottom edge at every detent, rendered only while the sheet is
    presented;
  - in a BottomSheetSwitcher, each sheet's positioner holds its panel at the
    block end and hides a sheet that is not presented, so one dialog can
    switch between sheets.
- The handle's pill is a pseudo-element and a standalone sheet's dialog holds
  the panel in place, so a standalone sheet's frame has no positioner or pill
  element of its own.
- Elements a presenting component adds beyond this frame are its own to
  justify.
- **PR1 — One stack per document.** Sheets rendered by every copy of the
  package loaded on a page that shares the stack protocol, and by every React
  root on it, form one stack per document: their order is their open order,
  whichever copy or root rendered them. The protocol version is part of the
  document-global key the copies meet under; a copy on another protocol
  version keeps a stack of its own, ordering only the sheets it renders, so
  two copies of different versions on one page are two independent stacks
  and neither reads the other's state. No public API reaches the ordering.
- **PR2 — No per-level backdrop cost.** A stack paints one visible backdrop
  whatever its depth — the scrim owner's, the bottom open sheet's under FR6 —
  and every other backdrop in the stack, a covered sheet's or a sheet's above
  the owner, is transparent, not removed (a switcher flow's dialogs likewise,
  `component:BottomSheetSwitcher` FR10).

## Accessibility contract

- **AR1 — Keyboard scroll path.** The named body is a tab stop while effectively
  overflowing. On forward Tab entry, the shared hook may delegate to the first
  sequential native link/button outside navigation-key-owning surfaces. It keeps
  the body stop for excluded content and skips the body on reverse traversal
  from the delegated first child.
- **AR2 — Focus continuity.** Content, overflow, or eligibility changes MUST NOT
  move focus. Pointer/programmatic body focus never delegates. A focused body
  losing overflow remains programmatically focusable.
- **AR3 — Covered and replaced sheets are not exposed.** A covered sheet that
  contains no presented sheet, and a replaced sheet through its handoff, are
  `inert` and `aria-hidden="true"`; the top or entering sheet is the only
  exposed dialog. A covered sheet that contains the top sheet is left alone,
  because the top sheet's modality already excludes it (FR6, FR10). Each
  sheet keeps its own role and accessible name; a non-modal sheet's manual
  popover host changes neither.
- **AR4 — Focus follows the person through a stack.** Opening the top sheet
  moves focus into it; a replace moves focus into the entering step whatever
  the native host did; a pop returns focus into the revealed sheet; the last
  close and a close of the whole stack return focus by FR9, and a sheet
  removed while open returns focus by FR20. A sheet that opens, on its own or
  pushed over another sheet, modal or not, focuses its first eligible
  `data-autofocus` descendant in every engine: one inside the sheet's own dialog, neither
  hidden nor inert, that takes focus when asked (`component:Dialog` FR2's
  eligibility). Where the engine's native focusing steps landed first (the
  panel in Chromium, the first control in Firefox) does not count as placed.
  Without an eligible descendant, focus stays where those steps landed: in
  Chromium the panel, in Firefox the first focusable control in the content,
  in WebKit wherever its steps land; a non-modal sheet's manual-popover host
  lands where that engine's `show()` would have (FR11). A sheet entering as a
  replacement (FR10) has its focus placed after the native focusing steps,
  because they may have left it in the replaced sheet or on the entering
  panel: there a `data-autofocus` descendant is focused first, else the
  panel, so a step flow that opens on a field keeps the keyboard on it.

- **AR5 — What assistive technology perceives of a push, a pop, and a
  replace.** The stack adds no announcement of its own to a push or a pop:
  it fires no live region, so focus entering the top sheet (push) or
  returning into the revealed sheet (pop, FR9) is what a screen reader
  reports, and the covered sheet is hidden meanwhile (AR3). Messages the
  sheets' own content owes are not the stack's and are delivered by their
  owners' rules — a message pending in a closing sheet reaches the person
  through the sheet revealed beneath it (`spec:AST-086` FR14). A replace is perceived as focus
  entering the new step: focus lands on the entering step's `data-autofocus`
  field or else its panel (AR4), so the entering dialog's accessible name is
  announced as focus enters it, and the retained step is hidden from the
  first frame of the handoff (AR3); the stack announces nothing of the
  retained step's leaving.

## Decision log

### DEC-1 — BottomSheet adopts automatic shared keyboard ownership

**Reference:** `component:BottomSheet/DEC-1`
**Decider:** cixzhang, 2026-09-13

BottomSheet supplies its existing label to
`keyboardAccess.owner="contentOrViewport"`. The shared hook keeps the overflowing
body named and tabbable, delegating forward keyboard entry to a safe first native
link/button. Pointer/programmatic focus and excluded content keep the body path. BottomSheet retains `stickyContainment="always"` because the
body deliberately remains the sheet-local Sticky boundary.

A real flow-root content box supplies the hook's observation seam while preserving
representative margin, float, percentage-height, flex/grid-child, and hug-height
behavior.

### DEC-2 — Sheets stack by open order and replace on a same-update switch

**Reference:** `component:BottomSheet/DEC-2`
**Decider:** imdreamrunner, 2026-10-23

Every modal sheet keeps its own open state and its own native dialog. A sheet
opened while another modal sheet is open stacks on top of it: the stack is
ordered by open order with tree order deciding ties within one update, exactly
one backdrop is visible, covered sheets recede and are blocked, dismissal
reaches only the top sheet, and the native top layer is put in that order
before the first paint when the browser's own order would differ (FR5–FR7).
A sheet rendered in another sheet's content is part of it: it leaves and
returns with its host and its state stays the caller's (FR8). When the top
sheet closes in the same update that one other standalone sheet of the same
modality opens, from a control inside the closing sheet, the opening sheet
replaces it with the one handoff a step flow needs — entering above, the
retained sheet aligning down when taller and fading after both motions, one
steady scrim, focus into the entering step, the first opener kept for the
flow's close — so a multi-step flow is one standalone sheet per step and one
state value (FR10). Non-modal sheets are hosted in the top layer as manual
popovers, never stack, and replace each other the same way (FR11). A
`BottomSheetSwitcher` flow is one level (FR12). The ordering is one per
document across React roots and across the package copies that share the
stack protocol (PR1; a copy on another protocol version keeps its own stack),
because the coordinated thing — a document's top layer — is wider than any
React tree. Nothing here is public API; a lone modal sheet never notices the
stack.
Rejected: an ordered list of steps on the switcher (`activeSheets`), because
it changes the switcher's public contract, requires every level of a flow to
live under one switcher, and cannot order a sheet opened from elsewhere.
Rejected: a `<BottomSheetStack>` provider or shared dialog that composes
levels, because a React context cannot reach a second root, a second copy of
the package, or a widget mounted outside it, while module-level singletons
already own document-wide facts here (the dismissal stack, the modal outlet
registry); the only scoping a provider adds is a "same level" boundary, which
FR10's actor condition supplies without a wrapper.
Rejected: a `stacked` or `replaces` prop to opt a sheet in, because a lone
sheet is unaffected and one state value already says what a flow means.
Rejected: a page-level `z-index` on a non-modal sheet to order a replace,
because `spec:AST-027` forbids a new band; the top layer orders it.

### DEC-3 — A pull past the top stretches on every pull

**Reference:** `component:BottomSheet/DEC-3`
**Decider:** the component owner, 2026-11-02

A pull up past the tallest detent stretches the sheet on the rubber band every
time, with no cap and no hold, and the page under the sheet never shows: the
surface the sheet keeps below the screen is deep enough for the whole stretch
and the release (FR18). A pull on the sheet's body counts as a pull on the
sheet once its content has no room left to scroll that way. So short content
stretches on the first pull and on every pull after it, and a long list scrolls
to its end before the sheet stretches. The evidence is an owner screen
recording on an iPhone, 2026-11-02, in which most body pulls on short content
moved nothing.
Rejected: a stretch capped at a 48 px reserve, because it holds still partway
through a pull.
Rejected: a second mechanism for upward pulls, because the existing pull path
already carries them; it only gave too many of them to the content.

### DEC-4 — A touch release takes UIKit's sheet values

**Reference:** `component:BottomSheet/DEC-4`
**Decider:** the component owner, 2026-11-02 and 2026-11-03; the spec owners,
2026-11-05

A touch release faster than about 1,000 px/s is a flick, and a flick goes at
least to the next position in its direction, as UIKit's sheet measures it:
1,008 px/s, with no more travel than a drag's first 8 px. So a short fast
flick at the lowest detent closes the sheet, where the projection alone
springs it back (FR19). A sheet a touch drag closes keeps the drag's speed: it
leaves on its close spring, starting at the release speed.

On a sheet with snap points, closing it with a flick takes a harder flick than
moving it to the next snap point. A flick lands on the first position at or
past its projection, 0.1 s of its velocity. So a flick above the lowest detent
closes the sheet once its projection passes the lowest detent, and a softer
one stops at the next detent. UIKit fits 164 measured releases with any
projection from 0.092 to 0.104 s. On an iPhone 17 Pro, with medium 342 pt and
gone 812 pt below large, a flick released at large closes from about 3,420
pt/s.

On 2026-11-03 the owner also limited the flick and the close spring to touch,
because a short mouse drag that closes a sheet changes how it works on a
desktop; a mouse or pen keeps its release. The spec owners then chose UIKit's
measured model for every touch release, not for flicks alone, so a faster
release never travels less than a slower one. A touch release moving back
toward where the drag began still cancels, as `spec:AST-094` FR37 requires,
where UIKit's sheet settles it by its landing.

Rejected: projecting the release only, because a short fast flick then
springs back although the hand meant to close the sheet. Rejected: a fixed
multiple of the flick speed for a close from above the lowest detent, because
the projection decides it. Rejected: closing from above the lowest detent only
when the projection lands nearer closed than any detent, because a flick past
the next detent goes on. Rejected: UIKit's projection for flicks
alone, because a release just under the flick speed then travels further than
one just over it.

# BottomSheetSwitcher component contract

## Intent

BottomSheetSwitcher coordinates a mutually exclusive sequence of BottomSheet
children, each presented in its own native dialog (DEC-1), without adding a
prop or changing a default. Its public lifecycle state is `deprecated`
(`spec:AST-017` FR26, DEC-3, DEP-0015): it is supported and maintained, every
requirement in this record stays in force, and the replacement — one standalone
`BottomSheet` per step driven by one state value, which `component:BottomSheet`
FR10 replaces in place when the step changes from inside the open step — is
what maintained docs, templates, stories, and agent guidance teach. Removal
happens only in a future declared minor through a `[breaking]` change. The
modality, identifier, and theming questions below stay open.

## Compatibility and migration

- Released default preserved: `yes`
- Compatibility class: nonbreaking; public props, defaults, exports, visual
  output, and transition behavior stay unchanged, except that under reduced
  motion a handoff follows `design:reduced-motion` (FR3), a visual-only motion
  change under `spec:AST-017` FR3. The element the `ref` resolves
  to, and that it changes on a handoff, is implementation rather than public
  contract (DEC-2). A switcher flow presented on its own behaves as released,
  with three fixes: a toast raised from a step renders above the flow and can
  be pressed (FR10, `component:BottomSheet` FR13), where it rendered behind the
  flow before; a flow whose active sheet is set on the server renders closed
  until hydration, then opens that sheet settled, with no entry motion
  (`component:BottomSheet` FR17), where it painted open over a live page
  before; and a step that opens with an eligible `data-autofocus` field (a
  `TextInput` with `hasAutoFocus`) focuses that field in every engine
  (`component:BottomSheet` AR4), where Chromium focused the step's panel
  before.
  A switcher flow presented while other sheets are open is one level of the
  stack `component:BottomSheet` FR12 describes; that is behavior of the stack,
  not a change to the switcher's own contract
- Lifecycle: `deprecated` (DEC-3, DEP-0015). Deprecation adds no production
  runtime behavior and no warning in a rendered application (`spec:AST-017`
  FR29); the `@deprecated` metadata lives on the TypeScript declarations of
  `BottomSheetSwitcher`, `BottomSheetSwitcherProps`, and `BottomSheet`'s
  `sheetId`, and the consumer docs carry the replacement route (FR29a, FR29b).
- Controlled/uncontrolled behavior: `activeSheet` remains fully controlled
- Migration decision: DEC-3; the migration is DEP-0015's

Consumer migration instructions belong in consumer docs and release notes.

## Ownership boundary

**Owns**

- The presentation lifecycle of every directly nested BottomSheet child's native
  dialog: which is presented, retained during a handoff, or closed.
- Which child sheet is active, retained during a handoff, or hidden.
- Which sheet dialog hosts the flow (its ref, `id`, and dismissal
  registration) and which paints the flow's one scrim, plus
  Escape/platform-close routing and final focus return for the flow. Focus
  containment is the native modal dialog's; the flow's one page scroll lock is
  held for the whole modal flow (DEC-1).
- The flow wrapper: the isolated surface that orders non-modal sheet dialogs
  locally (`spec:AST-027` FR2).

**Does not own / non-goals**

- A child sheet's panel, content, handle, height, snap points, swipe mechanics, or
  local visual styling — owned by `component:BottomSheet`.
- Page-level stacking or clipping escape — owned by
  `architecture:layer-runtime` and `spec:AST-027`.
- The stack of presented sheets, its order, its one visible scrim, and the
  replace handoff between standalone sheets — owned by `component:BottomSheet`
  DEC-2. A switcher flow takes part as one level (FR10 here, FR12 there); it
  does not order itself against other sheets.

### Transformation and precedence order

- **ORD1 — Handoff completion.** Select next child → mark previous child retained
  and inert → start entering transform and any required retained alignment → wait
  for both transforms → fade retained child → hide it. Under reduced motion the
  transform steps are skipped and the entering and retained children cross-fade
  or change at once (`design:reduced-motion` DR2, DR3), keeping this order.
- **ORD2 — Label precedence.** Consumer `aria-label` wins; otherwise consumer
  `aria-labelledby` wins; otherwise the active or retained child label names the
  dialog.

### Performance and resources

- **PR1 — One painted scrim.** In a modal flow, at rest exactly one sheet
  dialog is presented; during a handoff two are, with one backdrop painted.
  Native modality holds across a modal flow's child handoffs because a sheet
  dialog is modal throughout, and the one page scroll lock is held for the
  whole flow rather than per step. A non-modal flow paints no backdrop and
  holds no lock.
- **PR2 — Stable child registration.** Parent rerenders and changing consumer ref
  identities do not unregister a mounted child or cancel an active handoff.

## Accessibility contract

- **AR1 — Dialog semantics.** Modal presentations expose `aria-modal`; a required
  child changes the implicit dialog role to `alertdialog`; every visible flow has
  a consumer-provided or active-child-derived accessible name.
- **AR2 — Focus and inertness.** Modal focus remains inside the dialog, retained
  children are inert and `aria-hidden`, and focus returns after the final exit.
- **AR3 — Ordered dismissal.** Escape and platform close follow
  `family:overlay-dismissal`, including IME protection and topmost-layer ordering.

## Decision log

### DEC-1 — Each step owns its native dialog; the switcher coordinates

**Reference:** `component:BottomSheetSwitcher/DEC-1`
**Decider:** imdreamrunner, 2026-10-20

A switcher step is a BottomSheet with its own native dialog, presented and
closed like a standalone sheet, so one DOM shape serves standalone sheets and
switcher steps alike and the browser supplies modality per step: an inert
page, focus containment, `cancel` for Escape and the platform close, and the
backdrop. The switcher owns only what no single dialog can decide: which dialog
is presented, retained, or closed; the one scrim handed from the retained
dialog to the entering one without a fade; re-presenting a sheet that is still
open beneath a newer one when the flow returns to it; focus kept in the active
sheet when a retained dialog closes; focus returned to the flow's opener;
`cancel` gated by the active step's `purpose` through the shared dismissal
stack; and the flow's one page scroll lock, held from the moment a modal flow
is presented until after its exit, so a handoff between two open dialogs never
releases and re-acquires it. The switcher's one element is a flow wrapper with
no box in a modal flow and, in a non-modal flow, the isolated surface inside
which the sheet dialogs order themselves with local values.
Rejected: one switcher-owned dialog hosting every step as a panel, because a
step is then not a sheet and every standalone-sheet mechanism needs a second,
switcher-only implementation.
Rejected: a script focus trap on top of native containment, because the
browser already contains focus in a modal dialog and the trap fights its
inertness.
Rejected: a page-level `z-index` on a non-modal step's dialog, because
`spec:AST-027` FR4 forbids a new page band; the wrapper is the isolation owner.

### DEC-2 — The host dialog's identity is implementation, not contract

**Reference:** `component:BottomSheetSwitcher/DEC-2`
**Decider:** imdreamrunner, 2026-10-20

The `ref`, `id`, and `data-testid` of the switcher reach the dialog hosting
the flow: the active sheet's, the exiting sheet's through its exit, and, while
the flow is closed, the dialog that hosted it last or the first mounted
sheet's, so an object ref is never `null` while a sheet is mounted and the
identifying attributes appear once. Which element that is, and that it changes
on a handoff, is implementation. The released documentation (v0.7.1
`BottomSheetSwitcher.doc.mjs`) describes the ref as "Ref forwarded to the one
shared native dialog." and the switcher as "Its ref and shared DOM props
target that dialog." Code that copies `ref.current` once at mount keeps a
real `<dialog>`, but after a handoff that element may no longer be the host;
read `ref.current` at use time, or use a callback ref, to follow the flow. DOM
props and handlers given to the switcher (`className`, `style`, `onCancel`,
`onToggle`, `aria-*`, and the rest) reach every sheet dialog, with `id` and
`data-testid` on the host only, so a handler fires for whichever sheet dialog
raises the event. The owner rules this hosting — the moving host and the
per-dialog props alike — nonbreaking under `spec:AST-017` FR3 because no
visual output and no public API surface changes.
Rejected: classifying the moving ref as a breaking change, because no public
API, documented interaction, or visual output changes.

### DEC-3 — The switcher is deprecated in favour of standalone step sheets

**Reference:** `component:BottomSheetSwitcher/DEC-3`
**Decider:** imdreamrunner, 2026-10-23

A multi-step flow is written as one standalone `BottomSheet` per step driven
by one state value; closing the open step and opening the next in the same
update, from a control inside the open step or from code while focus is in
it, runs the handoff the switcher runs between its steps
(`component:BottomSheet` FR10), with no `sheetId` and no wrapper, for modal
and non-modal flows alike. The one case the replacement treats differently is
a step changed by a control outside the flow's sheets — a page control
beside a non-modal flow, or code acting while focus is on a page control —
which the switcher hands off and the standalone sheets close and open with
their own motion, because from outside the sheets a close and an open are two
sheets' own changes, not one person's move between steps; code acting while
focus is in the step or on the document body keeps the handoff
(`component:BottomSheet` FR10: the actor is inside the step, or none). The switcher therefore has no
job of its own left
and is `deprecated` (DEP-0015): it keeps working as released, every
requirement of this record stays in force, its declarations carry
`@deprecated`, maintained docs and templates teach the replacement, and no
production runtime behavior changes. The removal is a `[breaking]` change of a
future declared minor (`spec:AST-017` FR28, FR31) and is not decided here.
Rejected: keeping the switcher as the one supported flow API, because the
same handoff then needs two implementations and callers must choose between
two mental models for one UI.
Rejected: removing the switcher in the same release that ships the
replacement, because a patch cannot carry a `[breaking]` change
(`spec:AST-017` FR25, FR31) and released flows must keep working.
Rejected: a development-mode warning on render, because a whole-component
deprecation projects through its declarations and docs (`spec:AST-017` FR29).
